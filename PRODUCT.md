# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Noah Austin's personal site — a single-page CV/portfolio for a Grade 10 UWC student, debater, and aspiring rationalist. Three audiences, in priority order:

- **Peers** (fellow high school students) — browsing casually, phone-first, quick to sense whether something is trying too hard.
- **Prospective internship employers** — scanning for substance and signal: is this person a serious, capable thinker worth taking on?
- **College admissions officers** (secondary) — reading for intellectual character and genuine curiosity behind the credentials.

The job to be done: in under a minute, convince a skeptical reader that this is a real, disciplined young thinker — then let anyone who wants to go deeper read top-to-bottom.

## Product Purpose

A working document of who Noah is and what he's done, framed as an argument rather than a brochure. Success is a reader coming away thinking "this person actually thinks for themselves" — not "nice website." The design must carry intellectual credibility for adults while never reading as stiff or self-important to peers.

## Brand Personality

Rigorous and serious — the site should read as the work of a genuine first-principles thinker who earns intellectual respect through substance, not flash. Three words: **disciplined, curious, unpretentious.** Confident without overselling; the accomplishments are stated plainly and allowed to speak. The voice is that of someone comfortable being judged on the quality of their reasoning.

## Anti-references

The site must NOT look or feel like any of these:

- **Generic AI/template slop** — dark background + single neon accent, glassmorphism cards, mesh gradients, blur orbs, glow shadows, gradient text, decorative `01/02/03` numbering, animated counters, Inter + Space Grotesk. (This was the explicit failure mode of the original build.)
- **Corporate SaaS landing page** — hero metric, feature-card grids, CTA buttons everywhere, marketing energy.
- **Flashy teen portfolio** — over-animation, loud colors, gaming/streamer aesthetic, trying too hard to impress peers.
- **Stuffy academic CV** — a Times New Roman PDF dropped on a webpage, zero personality, respectable but forgettable.

The line to walk: serious enough for employers and admissions, alive enough that a peer doesn't bounce, human enough that it never feels like a résumé.

## Design Principles

1. **Argument, not brochure.** Structure the page as a line of reasoning (a premise, then evidence), not a list of achievements. Every section earns its place by supporting the thesis.
2. **Substance over signal.** Restraint reads as confidence. Cut anything decorative that doesn't carry meaning; let the content and typography do the work.
3. **Thinking made visible.** The distinctive device — margin notes, annotations, an editorial voice — should feel like watching a mind work, not like UI chrome.
4. **Earn the adult's trust without losing the peer.** Credibility for employers/admissions and approachability for students are the same target hit two ways: honest, plain, unpretentious.
5. **Every claim is real.** All factual content (name, tagline, bio, interests, activities, distinctions, links) is authoritative and preserved verbatim from `content/site.json`; design never invents or embellishes it.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA baseline: body text ≥4.5:1 contrast (large text ≥3:1), full keyboard navigation, visible focus states, and `prefers-reduced-motion` honored on every animation. Semantic HTML and meaningful link text throughout. Layout must remain fully readable and functional down to mobile widths.
